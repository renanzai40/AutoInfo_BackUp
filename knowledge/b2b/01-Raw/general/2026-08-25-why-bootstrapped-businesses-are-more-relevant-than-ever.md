## Original Content
The traditional VC model funds a risky search for product-market fit, argues guest author Richard de Silva of Lateral Investment Management, while bootstrapped founders typically start with known customer problems, prioritize profitability and grow steadily from revenue rather than outside capital.

## Summary
Bootstrapped businesses are becoming more relevant as they focus on known customer problems, prioritize profitability, and grow steadily from revenue, contrasting with traditional venture capital models that fund uncertain product-market fit searches.

## Key Points
- Traditional VC model funds risky searches for product-market fit
- Bootstrapped founders start with known customer problems
- Prioritization of profitability over rapid growth
- Steady growth from revenue rather than external capital
- Bootstrapped businesses are increasingly relevant in the current economic landscape
