## Summary
A French court has recognized the breast cancer of Air France flight attendant Sophie Lainault as an occupational disease, marking a first in the country. The decision was based on her exposure to night work, ionizing radiation, and passive smoking. Lainault, aged 59, hopes this ruling will pave the way for similar cases involving other women.

## Key Points
- Breast cancer was legally acknowledged as an occupational disease for a flight attendant in France for the first time.
- The court identified night work, ionizing radiation, and passive smoking as key occupational risk factors contributing to the disease.
- The case involves Sophie Lainault, a 59-year-old Air France employee, setting a precedent in occupational health law.
- This ruling may lead to increased recognition and prevention of work-related health hazards in the aviation industry.
- It underscores the importance of legal and medical frameworks in addressing occupational exposures and their health impacts.
