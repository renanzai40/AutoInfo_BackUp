## Original Content
PURPOSE: The introduction of intraoperative CT (iCT) has provided a new tool that may improve outcomes in the surgical management of maxillofacial fractures (MFFs). This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the clinical value of iCT in MFFs and identified the fracture types and circumstances in which it provides notable benefit.
METHODS: PubMed, Scopus, and ClinicalTrials.gov were searched for clinical studies published or registered from January 2020 to November 2025. Random-effect models pooled the intraoperative CT revision rate and post-operative bilateral orbital volume difference across various fracture types.
RESULTS: Eighteen studies (1165 patients; 12 cohorts, 1 case-control, and 5 case series) were included across zygomaticomaxillary complex, orbital, mandibular, and nasal fractures. CONCLUSIONS: Overall, iCT appeared most valuable when 3-dimensional reduction accuracy or implant seating could not be confidently assessed before closure, especially in orbital reconstruction and anatomically constrained, symmetry-critical MFFs.

## Summary
This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the clinical value of intraoperative CT (iCT) in maxillofacial fracture surgery. Pooled analysis showed a 36% intraoperative revision rate, indicating iCT is most useful when 3D reduction accuracy or implant seating cannot be confidently assessed, especially in orbital reconstruction and symmetry-critical fractures.

## Key Points
- 18 studies with 1165 patients were included across zygomaticomaxillary complex, orbital, mandibular, and nasal fractures.
- The pooled intraoperative CT revision rate was 36% from 11 studies with 493 patients.
- iCT was most valuable for orbital reconstruction and anatomically constrained, symmetry-critical maxillofacial fractures.
- iCT is recommended when 3-dimensional reduction accuracy or implant seating cannot be confidently assessed before closure.
- The review searched PubMed, Scopus, and ClinicalTrials.gov from January 2020 to November 2025.
