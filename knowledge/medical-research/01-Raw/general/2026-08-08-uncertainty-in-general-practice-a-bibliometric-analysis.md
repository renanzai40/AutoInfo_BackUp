## Original Content
BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Uncertainty is often considered a defining feature of general practice, yet empirical comparisons across medical specialties are limited. This study investigates whether uncertainty is uniquely prevalent in general practice or a commonality of clinical work across medical disciplines.
METHOD: A bibliometric analysis of 3757 articles from PubMed (1966-2025) was conducted. A total of 287 relevant studies were categorised by specialty and study type to assess thematic focus and empirical content.
RESULTS: Publications on uncertainty were comparable in number between general practice (n = 87) and non-general practice specialties (n = 94), with general practice publications showing a higher proportion of empirical studies (67% vs 61%). A recent acceleration in non-general practice literature suggests growing interdisciplinary interest.
DISCUSSION: Findings challenge the narrative of general practice exceptionalism regarding managing uncertainty, suggesting that uncertainty is a systemic feature of medical practice. This has implications for medical education, professional identity and cross-specialty collaboration.

## Summary
This bibliometric analysis of 287 relevant studies found comparable numbers of publications on uncertainty in general practice (n=87) and other specialties (n=94), with general practice having a slightly higher proportion of empirical studies. The findings challenge the notion that uncertainty is unique to general practice, suggesting it is a systemic feature of medical practice.

## Key Points
- Publication counts on uncertainty were similar between general practice (n=87) and non-general practice specialties (n=94).
- General practice publications had a higher proportion of empirical studies (67%) compared to non-general practice (61%).
- A recent acceleration in non-general practice literature indicates growing interdisciplinary interest in uncertainty.
- Findings challenge the narrative of general practice exceptionalism regarding uncertainty management.
- The study implies uncertainty is a systemic feature of medical practice with implications for education and collaboration.
