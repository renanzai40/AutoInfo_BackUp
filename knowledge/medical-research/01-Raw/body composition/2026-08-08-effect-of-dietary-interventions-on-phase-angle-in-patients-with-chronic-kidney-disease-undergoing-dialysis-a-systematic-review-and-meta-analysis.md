## Original Content
CONTEXT: Phase angle (PhA), derived from bioelectrical impedance analysis, is inversely associated with malnutrition, a common condition in chronic kidney disease (CKD) managed through dietary therapy. Although evidence from oncology and sports medicine suggests nutritional interventions may improve PhA, supporting its potential use as a marker of therapeutic efficacy, data from CKD remain limited.
OBJECTIVE: The impact of nutritional-related interventions on PhA values in patients with CKD and undergoing dialysis was evaluated in this systematic review and meta-analysis.
DATA SOURCES: A systematic literature search for clinical trial studies was conducted in the PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, CINAHL, and Embase databases up to September 2025.
DATA EXTRACTION: Eligible clinical trials included adults with CKD and undergoing dialysis, applying any dietetic intervention as compared with a control, and reporting PhA variations. Meta-analysis was conducted only on data from randomized clinical trials (RCTs) and analyzed by stratification according to intervention details.
DATA ANALYSIS: A total of 14 studies (n = 9 RCTs) comprising 969 participants were included in the review. Overall, there was no influence of dietetic intervention accompanied or not by an exercise-related component, compared with standard of care (ie, no intervention), on PhA values (mean difference [MD] = 0.22; 95% CI, -0.20 to 0.63; P < .001; I2 = 78%), nor did the isolated effect of the dietary component (MD = 0.26; 95% CI, -0.22 to 0.73; P < .001; I2 = 80%) or use of amino acids or a protein-based unimodal intervention (MD = 0.49; 95% CI, -0.19 to 1.17; P < .001; I2 = 73%) have an impact.
CONCLUSION: Our meta-analysis did not show influences of dietetic intervention on PhA among patients with CKD who are undergoing dialysis. However, given low number of RCTs and important risk of bias, high-quality RCTs are needed to verify our findings.
SYSTEMATIC REVIEW REGISTRATION: PROSPERO no. CRD42024601056.

## Summary
This systematic review and meta-analysis assessed the impact of dietary interventions on phase angle (PhA) in adults with chronic kidney disease undergoing dialysis. Analysis of 14 studies (9 RCTs, 969 participants) found no significant effect of dietetic interventions on PhA, whether combined with exercise, isolated dietary changes, or amino acid/protein supplementation. The authors call for high-quality randomized controlled trials to confirm these findings due to the limited number of RCTs and risk of bias.

## Key Points
- Dietetic interventions did not significantly affect phase angle in dialysis patients with CKD (mean difference 0.22; 95% CI, -0.20 to 0.63).
- No effect was seen for the isolated dietary component (MD 0.26) or amino acid/protein-based interventions (MD 0.49).
- The review included 14 studies, of which 9 were randomized clinical trials, totaling 969 participants.
- Evidence from oncology and sports medicine suggests potential benefits of nutritional interventions on PhA, but CKD data remain limited.
- The study is registered as PROSPERO CRD42024601056.
