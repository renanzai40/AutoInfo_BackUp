## Original Content
RESEARCH QUESTION: Does the time elapsed between planned oocyte cryopreservation cycles affect oocyte yield at the second retrieval?
DESIGN: This retrospective cohort study included 2412 patients undergoing at least two oocyte retrievals within 180 days for elective oocyte cryopreservation between 2010 and 2022. The primary outcome was mature oocyte yield in the second cycle. Secondary outcomes included changes in total oocyte yield, stimulation days, and gonadotrophin dose (IU) required in the second cycle, and frequency of cancellation of the second cycle.
RESULTS: Patients were categorized by time elapsed between stimulation cycles: immediate second cycle (≤28 days, n = 399), standard timing (29-90 days, n = 1448), or delayed second cycle (91-180 days, n = 565). After adjusting for age, mature oocyte yield at first stimulation, protocol, and trigger medication, mature oocyte yield at the second retrieval was 9% lower for the immediate group (rate ratio 0.91, 95% CI 0.86-0.96) and 7% lower for the delayed group (rate ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.89-0.98) compared with standard timing. These differences did not meet the prespecified criteria for inferiority, defined as a 20% decrease in oocyte yield. The number of days of stimulation did not differ by interval group. Total gonadotrophin dose was significantly higher for patients who underwent their second cycle immediately compared with those who underwent their second cycle with standard timing (mean difference 187 IU, 95% CI 35-339).
CONCLUSIONS: Compared with waiting 1-3 months before the second stimulation for planned oocyte cryopreservation, shorter or longer intervals (up to 180 days) are non-inferior, although associated with a small decrease in oocyte yield. The clinical relevance of these findings may vary depending on patient scenario.

## Summary
A retrospective cohort study of 2412 patients undergoing planned oocyte cryopreservation found that compared with a 29-90 day interval between stimulation cycles, shorter (≤28 days) or longer (91-180 days) intervals resulted in slightly lower mature oocyte yield (9% and 7% respectively), but these were not inferior per the prespecified 20% threshold. Immediate second cycles required higher gonadotrophin doses. Overall, intercycle intervals up to 180 days are non-inferior for oocyte yield.

## Key Points
- Immediate (≤28 days) and delayed (91-180 days) second cycles yielded 9% and 7% lower mature oocyte yield, respectively, vs standard 29-90 days.
- Differences did not meet predefined inferiority threshold of 20% decrease in oocyte yield.
- Immediate second cycle required significantly higher total gonadotrophin dose (mean difference 187 IU).
- Stimulation duration did not differ across interval groups.
- Findings suggest flexibility in timing for planned oocyte cryopreservation, with clinical relevance depending on patient scenario.
