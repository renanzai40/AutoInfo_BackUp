## Original Content
Las leyes de Corea del Sur regulan los insectos que están permitidos en los platos de los establecimientos comerciales.

## Summary
A restaurant with two Michelin stars in South Korea is facing up to a year in prison for serving a sorbet with ants as dessert. South Korean laws strictly regulate which insects are permitted in commercial food establishments, emphasizing food safety compliance.

## Key Points
- The restaurant served ants in a dessert, leading to potential legal penalties.
- South Korea enforces laws that restrict the use of certain insects in food to ensure safety.
- Violations of these regulations can result in imprisonment for up to one year.
- The incident highlights the intersection of culinary creativity and regulatory standards in high-end dining.
- This case underscores the importance of adhering to national food safety laws even in prestigious establishments.
