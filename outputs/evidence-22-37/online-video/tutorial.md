### AI Transforming the Music Industry

Artificial intelligence is revolutionizing the music industry, from production to distribution. For example, rapper Tyga recently used AI to create his album $tarface, stating he doesn't care about the method, drawing parallels to the introduction of Auto-Tune (Source: https://pitchfork.com/story/tyga-admits-to-making-new-album-dollartarface-with-ai-i-dont-care/). This highlights the growing acceptance of technology in art, enabling new forms of creative expression. The Billboard charts are adapting to include AI-generated music, with the platform affirming its commitment to transparency to maintain chart integrity and ensure fair representation (Source: https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/ai-generated-music-billboard-transparency-1236323619/). Such transparency is crucial as AI becomes more integrated into music creation, helping audiences and industry professionals understand the origins of content.

Beyond production, AI is influencing how music is consumed and monetized. Stability AI, a company focused on generative AI, has raised $76 million in funding to develop tools for music and content creation, backed by major labels like Universal and Warner Music Group (Source: https://variety.com/2026/biz/news/stability-ai-raises-76-million-funding-round-1236842351/). This investment underscores the industry's bet on AI as a key driver of innovation, potentially democratizing music production but also raising questions about copyright and the role of human creativity. As AI tools become more accessible, students can explore how technology reshapes artistic processes and industry economics.



### Technology Behind Modern Streaming Services

Streaming platforms like Netflix leverage advanced AI and data technologies to enhance user experience. One example is GenRec, an LLM-native recommendation system that uses large language models to personalize content suggestions, matching or exceeding traditional systems while requiring fewer labeled examples (Source: https://netflixtechblog.com/genrec-towards-llm-native-recommendation-at-netflix-f20be6f643e3). This approach verbalizes user histories as text, making recommendations more intuitive and scalable, which is essential for platforms with vast content libraries.

On the backend, Netflix has built complex systems to handle massive scale, such as a real-time distributed graph for querying relationships between billions of data points with sub-100ms latency (Source: https://netflixtechblog.com/how-and-why-netflix-built-a-real-time-distributed-graph-part-3-querying-the-graph-with-grpc-0f3468349607). Additionally, their in-house LLM serving platform allows them to run AI models internally, using tools like vLLM for efficient deployment (Source: https://netflixtechblog.com/in-house-llm-serving-at-netflix-a5a8e799ea2c). These technologies ensure that streaming services can deliver personalized content quickly and reliably, demonstrating the intersection of AI, data engineering, and user interface design in modern media.
